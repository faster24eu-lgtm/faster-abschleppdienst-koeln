## Round 0 (2026-10-18, unprompted)

- **Jaguar (most models):** a lever/cable **under the engine cover**. You pull the little cable back and that puts the gearbox in neutral, so the car can be towed.
- **Land Rover, Range Rover Evoque:** same as Jaguar, under the engine cover.
- **Range Rover Velar:** same, under the engine cover.
- **Range Rover Sport:** in the **cup holder in the centre console**. Remove the bottom of the cup holder, and there's a lever underneath.
- **BMW and Mini:** "the screw that you unscrew on the bottom" (to clarify).
- **Iveco Daily:** in the **floor, on the right side of the seat**. It's covered; remove the cover by hand and pull back the lever.
- **Mercedes Sprinter (about 2009–2015):** a **round cover right under the gear knob**. Pop it out with a screwdriver, then press the key in.

## Round 1 (2026-10-18): electric parking brake (EPB)

- Some older cars have a **mechanical emergency release lever** for the EPB. Example: **Renault Scénic**, a **yellow lever** in the **centre console** or sometimes in the **boot**. (Owner: "vague memory, double check".)
- The first generations of EPB had a way to release it by hand. **Newer cars have no manual release.**
- The owner's practice with newer cars: use **dollies (Radroller) or a crane**. The alternative is removing the wheel and powering the EPB actuator directly with 12 V (plus/minus) so it opens. That is workshop-level and not for customers.
- The owner wants me to **research and double-check everything**, and only ask him about gaps.

## Research log (verified = official owner handbook / workshop source)

### Jaguar: VERIFIED (JLR owner handbooks via topix.jaguar.jlrext.com)
- **XF (X250) AWD:** the park release cable is in the **engine bay, left side of the engine**. Remove the engine cover (lift at the front, slide it forward). Take the park release tool from the toolkit under the boot floor, unclip the cable from the engine, press 2 tangs to remove the blanking plug, and fit the tool on the cable. Then lift the strap, turn the locking device 90° anticlockwise and pull the lever up until it latches vertical, which gives N. Reset: push the latch out, lever horizontal, turn the lock 90° clockwise, disconnect the tool, refit the plug and the engine cover. (procedure 477675)
- **XF RWD:** the lever is in the **cubby box** (armrest). Lift the lid, prise up the trim panel, then strap / 90° anticlockwise / lever up until it latches. Same source.
- **F-Type:** in the **cubby box, left side trim panel**, same lever mechanism (procedure 446230).
- **XJ (X351):** **under the centre console between the drinks holder and the cubby box**. Remove the trim panel ahead of the cubby box, same lever (procedure 473895). Note: keep the Smart key in the car with the ignition on during recovery so the steering column stays unlocked.
- **All Jaguar procedures:** chock the wheels / apply the EPB / press the brake first. The recommended transport is on a transporter. Never tow on all four wheels.
- **Owner's statement "most Jaguars under the engine cover":** fits the AWD XF. Still TODO: XE, XF X260 (2016+), F-Pace, E-Pace, I-Pace.

### Land Rover
- **Discovery Sport (L550): VERIFIED** (handbook p.241–242, topix procedure 502600). The park release lever is **beneath the air cleaner in the engine bay**. Remove the air cleaner (clips, connector, bolt, intake pipe), turn the park release lever **anticlockwise to the forward position**, and lock it with a **5 mm pin** (Allen key / drill bit) through the hole in the lever into the gearbox casing. Secure the vehicle first (chocks, EPB, or a second person on the brake).
- **Evoque (L538), same platform (9-speed):** very likely the same as the Discovery Sport. The owner says "under the engine cover". TODO: confirm, plus the Evoque L551 (2019+) and the Velar.
- **Range Rover L405 / Sport L494:** forum posts quote the workshop manual: **under the drinks holder (cup holder) in the floor console**. Remove the trim at the base of the cupholder, turn the locking cylinder 90° anticlockwise, and pull the lever up until it locks vertical. Matches the owner's statement for the Sport. (rrsport.co.uk, fullfatrr.com, not official yet)
- **Range Rover Sport pre-2010 (L320):** forum: under the cupholder, unlock the plastic safety lock with a coin, then pull the red lever. The handbrake release is separate.
- **Land Rover recovery doc:** if N cannot be selected, the propshafts must be removed before towing, so use a flatbed or dollies.

### BMW: NOT YET VERIFIED
- Third-party tutorial (5 Series): prise off the cover at the base of the selector, then push a screwdriver into the hole on the left to release to N (older mechanical selector). Owner says "screw on the bottom". Clarify: older (E-series) vs newer electronic selector (F/G).

### Volvo: VERIFIED (volvocars.com support, snippets only; the pages return 403 to the fetcher)
- **V70 (older):** the selector must be released from P when the battery is dead. Lift the **rubber mat in the compartment behind the centre console**, where there's a **hole for the key blade** at the bottom.
- **V40:** remove the **cup-shaped insert in the centre console compartment**, then the key-blade hole.
- **XC40:** cars with the **7-speed (dual-clutch) gearbox must not be towed with wheels on the ground**, only on a platform. Automatics: max 80 km/h and 80 km, key in the car, ignition position II.

### Tesla: VERIFIED (tesla owner's manual, Model Y "Instructions for Transporters", Model 3 manual)
- **Tow Mode** on the touchscreen keeps the car in N with the parking brake off. It is only for winching onto a flatbed or moving out of a parking space, max ~5 km/h (region-dependent 5–8 km/h). It needs a key detected and the car **not plugged in**, and it **needs 12 V power**.
- **No 12 V:** Model 3: behind the front tow-eye cover there are a red and a black wire. Connect an external 12 V source (jump starter) for max 30 s; this **only opens the bonnet/frunk**, it doesn't charge the battery. Then you can reach the 12 V battery.
- **If Tow Mode isn't available:** Tesla says to use **self-loading dollies or tyre skates**.

### Mercedes: PARTLY (moba snippets)
- Mercedes recommends **transporting rather than towing**. Automatics may be towed only with both axles on the ground (when N is possible). No towing backwards.
- A van manual: towing with one axle raised is allowed only with the ignition on, max 5 km at 20 km/h, and should be done by a towing company.
- No mechanical park-lock release found for DIRECT SELECT (column) cars and vans, so power (jump-start) is needed, otherwise dollies.
