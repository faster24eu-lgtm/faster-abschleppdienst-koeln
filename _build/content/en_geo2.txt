<h2>A north-south axis through six states</h2>
<p>The A7 begins at the Ellund border crossing as a continuation of the Danish E45 and runs through Schleswig-Holstein, Hamburg, Lower Saxony, Hesse, Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg into the Allgäu. At 962.2 kilometres it's Germany's longest federal motorway, and after Spain's Autovía A-7, the second-longest continuous national motorway in Europe. Given its length, "on the A7" isn't enough for your location: a driver needs your direction of travel and a junction, an exit, or a nearby service station.</p>
<h2>Schleswig-Holstein: heathland, the high bridge, the Rendsburg interchange</h2>
<p>From Ellund, the four-lane route runs west of Flensburg towards Schleswig and past the Hüttener Berge nature park to Rendsburg. There it crosses the Kiel Canal and Rader Island on the Rader high bridge. At the Rendsburg interchange, the A210 connects to Kiel. South of it lies the Bordesholm triangle, where the A215 from Kiel joins only from and towards the south. From there the motorway has six lanes and bypasses Neumünster to the west. If you break down here in the open heathland, wind and little shelter are typical, see our guide (German) <a href="ratgeber-panne-autobahn.html">Breaking down on the motorway</a>.</p>
<h2>Hamburg: noise-protection tunnels, the Elbe Tunnel, the Elbmarsch elevated road</h2>
<p>The A7 reaches Hamburg in the Schnelsen district. It runs through a noise-protection tunnel about 500 metres long, joins the A23 at the Hamburg-Nordwest triangle, and passes through a second noise-protection tunnel of about 900 metres. The Langenfelde bridge crosses the railway tracks to Altona station. After that, the New Elbe Tunnel, with four tubes, two per direction, runs under the Elbe, the last fixed river crossing before the North Sea. South of it begins the Elbmarsch elevated road, at over four kilometres Germany's longest road bridge. At the Hamburg-Südwest triangle, the short A261 connects the A7 with the A1. More on Hamburg is on our page <a href="en-stadt-hamburg.html">Towing Service Hamburg</a>.</p>
<h2>Lower Saxony: the Lüneburg Heath and the Leine valley</h2>
<p>After the state border comes the complex junction of the Maschen interchange and the Horst triangle, where the A39 and A1 connect. The A7 then crosses the Lüneburg Heath; near Bad Fallingbostel it widens back to six lanes, and the A27 joins at the Walsrode triangle. Near Schwarmstedt, the Hannover metropolitan area comes into view: the A352 branches off at the Hannover-Nord triangle, followed shortly after by the interchanges with the A37 and A2. It passes Hildesheim, runs east of it onto the Vorholz, reaches the A39 at the Salzgitter triangle, and follows the Leine valley near Northeim. For callouts on the heath, distances between exits matter, so tell us the exit number.</p>
<h2>The Kaufunger Wald: gradients up to 8 percent</h2>
<p>Near Hann. Münden, the A7 crosses the Werra valley on a bridge 60 metres high, running parallel to the high-speed ICE rail line. A winding climb follows onto the main ridge of the Kaufunger Wald, with gradients up to 8 percent, one of the steepest motorway sections in Europe. On the other side, it descends in a straight line from the Großer Staufenberg to Kassel. In a breakdown on a gradient, pay special attention to your brakes and hazard lights, more in our guide <a href="en-ratgeber-bremsen-versagen.html">Brakes failing</a>.</p>
<h2>Hesse: Kassel, the Knüll, and the Rhön</h2>
<p>East of Kassel, the interchanges with the A49 and A44 follow in quick succession, with a large service area including a motorway restaurant and truck stop in between (Lohfeldener Rüssel). Past Homberg (Efze), the A7 runs steeply and winding through the Knüll mountains; the A4 branches east at the Kirchheim triangle, and the A5 begins at the Hattenbach triangle. Near Fulda, the town is bypassed to the east, and the A66 branches off at the Fulda triangle. A long climb onto the southern Rhön crosses the 96-metre-high Grenzwald bridge, the highest bridge along the entire route, shortly before the state border with Bavaria.</p>
<h2>Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg: the Main, the Frankenhöhe, the Alb</h2>
<p>On the Bavarian side follow Bad Brückenau, the Fränkische Saale valley near Hammelburg, and the Schweinfurt/Werneck interchange with the A70. At the Biebelried interchange, the A7 meets the A3. Near Marktbreit it crosses the Main on a bridge 60 metres high, reaches the Wörnitz valley near Rothenburg ob der Tauber, and meets the A6 at the Feuchtwangen/Crailsheim interchange. In Baden-Württemberg it crosses the Virngrund, passing beneath it in a tunnel about 470 metres long, climbs the Swabian Alb near Aalen through a second tunnel, and crosses the Härtsfeld. At the Ulm/Elchingen interchange it crosses the A8, and further south it passes Ulm and Neu-Ulm at the Hittistetten triangle. It continues along the Iller to Memmingen, where the A96 branches off, and on into the foothills of the Allgäu Alps.</p>

Tell us your direction of travel and the nearest junction, and we'll coordinate the help.</p><p><a class="btn btn-wa" href="@@WA@@" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Send location on WhatsApp</a> <a class="btn btn-y" href="tel:+4917641956993">+49 176 41956993</a></p></div></section>

<h2>From Ferch to Munich</h2>
<p>The A9 begins at the Potsdam triangle, about 45 kilometres south-west of central Berlin near Ferch, where it branches off the Berlin ring (A10). It crosses Brandenburg, Saxony-Anhalt, Saxony, Thuringia and Bavaria. Except for the main carriageway at the Hermsdorf interchange, the Nuremberg interchange, and the final stretch in Munich, it has at least three lanes per direction throughout. If you break down on the A9, it helps to tell us your direction of travel, the last junction, and the landscape you're in.</p>
<h2>Brandenburg and Saxony-Anhalt: pine forest, the Fläming, the Elbe</h2>
<p>The route starts amid extensive pine forests, passes the former Beelitz lung sanatorium grounds through the Baruth ice-age valley into the Fläming, a low, forested range of hills. Between Niemegk and the state border with Saxony-Anhalt, the route is winding and hilly. The listed Hagenbrücke, a fully brick-built arch bridge from the original construction period, crosses a small valley. Near Vockerode, the A9 crosses the Elbe, connects to Dessau, and bridges the Mulde. The dead-straight southbound section past Bitterfeld-Wolfen was already designed as a record-speed track in the 1930s. The driving feel remains: long straights, little room to manoeuvre.</p>
<h2>Saxony: the Schkeuditz interchange and the Mitteldeutsche Schleife</h2>
<p>At the Schkeuditz interchange, the A9 meets the A14, together forming, with the A38 and A143, a double motorway ring around Halle and Leipzig, the Mitteldeutsche Schleife. Leipzig/Halle Airport sits right next to the interchange. At the Rippachtal interchange, where the A38 connects, this ring section ends. More on the region is on our page <a href="en-stadt-leipzig.html">Towing Service Leipzig</a>.</p>
<h2>Thuringia: the Holzland, the Hermsdorf interchange, the Slate Mountains</h2>
<p>South of Weißenfels, the A9 leaves the North German Plain, crosses into Thuringia, and winds through the Thuringian Holzland near Eisenberg. The Hermsdorf interchange with the A4 is a major junction in Germany's motorway network. At the interchange, the A9's main carriageway still has four lanes, with the right-hand lane in each direction branching onto a parallel carriageway. Near Triptis, the route descends into the Orla valley, followed by the Thuringian Slate Mountains and, south of Schleiz, a winding, hilly stretch. At the Saale, the border between East and West Germany until 1990, the motorway crosses into Bavaria.</p>
<h2>Frankenwald, the Münchberg plateau, Bayreuth</h2>
<p>On the Bavarian side, the A9 climbs out of the Saale valley through numerous tight bends; the A72 branches east at the Bavarian Vogtland triangle. Near Münchberg follows a long straight through the Pulschnitz valley, then the Münchberg plateau between the Frankenwald and the Fichtelgebirge. Near Bad Berneck, a descent parallel to the steep Schiefe Ebene railway line leads into the valley of the White Main, crossed on a valley bridge more than a kilometre long. The A70 branches off at the Bayreuth/Kulmbach triangle. At the Bindlacher Berg, the motorway enters Bayreuth's city area, where widening to six lanes near Laineck created a noise-protection enclosure about 360 metres long. For a breakdown on a descent, see our guide <a href="en-ratgeber-bremsen-versagen.html">Brakes failing</a>.</p>
<h2>The Fränkische Alb: climbs, hillside bridges, Schnaittach</h2>
<p>South of Bayreuth follows a long climb onto the Fränkische Alb, crossing the Lindenhardt forest. Near Trockau begins the second, more winding part of the climb, close to the Kleiner Kulm, the highest point of Franconian Switzerland. Near Pegnitz, the route enters the Veldenstein forest. The main ridge of the Alb is crossed at about 560 metres above sea level. Past Hormersdorf, the descent into the Schnaittach valley begins at the Hienberg, with the carriageways running separately on opposite hillsides. The Schnaittach valley bridge is almost 1.3 kilometres long. Breaking down here can mean standing on a hillside bridge, which complicates rescue and towing. Tell us your direction of travel and the bridge's name if you know it.</p>
<h2>Nuremberg: the Nuremberg interchange and Nuremberg-Ost</h2>
<p>Near Lauf, the route crosses the Pegnitz, then comes the Nuremberg interchange with the A3. There, the main carriageway coming from the north joins the A3, which from this point forms the A9's route heading south. At the interchange, the A9 has only four lanes. It then runs through the Lorenzer Reichswald; the A6 crosses at the Nuremberg-Ost interchange, and the A73 joins at the Nuremberg/Feucht triangle.</p>
<h2>Ingolstadt, the Holledau, Munich</h2>
<p>Near Greding, the motorway reaches the Fränkische Alb ridge again. Near Kinding, after crossing the Altmühl, another climb begins. The Munich-bound carriageway, built later, runs on a hillside bridge, while the Nuremberg-bound carriageway uses the two original carriageways, with cars and trucks kept separate. Near Denkendorf lies the summit of the Fränkische Alb, followed by a descent through the Köschinger forest. Further junctions include the Holledau triangle and the Neufahrn interchange. At the end, the A9 leads into the Mittlerer Ring at the München-Schwabing exit, see <a href="en-stadt-muenchen.html">Towing Service Munich</a>.</p>
