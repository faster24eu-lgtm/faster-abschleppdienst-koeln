If you break down on it, you're on one of the busiest sections in Germany. <h2>From the Elten border crossing south and east</h2>
<p>The A3 begins at the former Elten border crossing as a continuation of the Dutch Rijksweg 12, initially running four lanes wide at some distance parallel to the Rhine. Past Eltenberg and Emmerich it follows the Dutch border through the sparsely populated right bank of the Lower Rhine, reaches the first city of the Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region at Wesel, and crosses the Lippe and the Wesel-Datteln canal near Hünxe. At the Oberhausen interchange, the A2 starts heading north and east, while the main carriageway continues into Oberhausen as the A516; drivers wanting to stay on the A3 need to change lanes. For your location, that means: tell us whether you're before or after the interchange.</p>
<h2>The Ruhr area: Oberhausen, Duisburg, Kaiserberg</h2>
<p>South of the Oberhausen interchange, the A3 has six lanes, and keeps that width, apart from short stretches at interchanges, all the way to Nuremberg. At the Oberhausen-West interchange it crosses the A42 (Emscherschnellweg); further south it crosses the Rhine-Herne canal and the Ruhr. The Kaiserberg interchange with the A40 (Ruhrschnellweg) is a complex layout with several ramps packed into a tight space, due to the acute angle and the parallel railway lines to Duisburg's main station. The motorway there passes through the grounds of Duisburg Zoo, which has a planted green bridge over it. The yellow Expo bridge now stands in Duisburg's city forest. At the Breitscheid interchange there are connections to the A52 and A524, and at the Ratingen-Ost interchange a section of the A44 ends, beginning the eastern part of Düsseldorf's motorway ring. More on the region: <a href="en-stadt-essen.html">Essen</a> and <a href="en-stadt-duesseldorf.html">Düsseldorf</a>.</p>
<h2>The Niederbergisches Land, Leverkusen, Cologne</h2>
<p>At the Hilden interchange, the A46 ends the eastern bypass of Düsseldorf. At the Langenfeld triangle, the A542 forms a cross-link to the A59. After the Wupper near Opladen, the Cologne area begins. The Leverkusen interchange with the A1 marks the start of Cologne's eastern motorway ring, and there the A3 has eight lanes, said to be the busiest motorway section in Germany. From the Cologne-Ost interchange it shares its carriageway with the A4; at the Cologne-Heumar triangle the A4 branches off again and the southernmost section of the A59 begins. More on this at <a href="en-pannenhilfe-a3-koeln.html">Breakdown on the A3 near Cologne</a>. If you break down in this section, it's worth knowing the steps in our guide <a href="en-ratgeber-panne-autobahn.html">Breaking down on the motorway</a>, since traffic is heavy.</p>
<h2>The Königsforst, the Agger valley, the Westerwald</h2>
<p>Past Heumar, the A3 runs through the Königsforst, reaching the Sülz valley near Rösrath and the Agger valley shortly before Lohmar. It curves past Cologne/Bonn Airport, which is only connected via the parallel A59, crosses the Sieg near Siegburg, and crosses the A560 at the Bonn/Siegburg interchange. From there to Frankfurt, the high-speed Cologne–Rhine/Main ICE rail line runs parallel. While the railway uses many bridges and tunnels, the A3 has numerous bends and gradients. Past the Siebengebirge, it climbs onto the heights of the Westerwald and crosses into Rhineland-Palatinate above Bad Honnef. At the highest point between Cologne and Frankfurt, near Willroth, the headframe of the former Georg iron-ore mine stands beside the route. At the Dernbach triangle the A48 joins; near Montabaur it crosses the B255, and at the Elzer Berg a notable descent leads into the Limburg basin. Around here the motorway crosses into Hesse.</p>
<h2>Limburg, the Taunus, the Wiesbaden interchange</h2>
<p>Past Limburg, the A3 crosses the Lahn valley on a high bridge and runs through the Idstein depression. Shortly before Idstein it climbs onto the Taunus ridge, crossing it at its narrowest point near Niedernhausen, followed by a long descent through the Vordertaunus into the Untermain plain. At the Wiesbaden interchange, the A3 connects with the A66; near Raunheim it crosses the Main for the first time. Past the bridge, the A67 branches off at the Mönchhof triangle. The A3 then passes through the grounds of Frankfurt Airport. Two taxiway bridges there carry aircraft over the motorway and the ICE line, and near Terminal 1 the airport's long-distance railway station stands right next to the motorway. Further east follows the Frankfurter Kreuz with the A5, see <a href="en-stadt-frankfurt.html">Towing Service Frankfurt</a>.</p>
<h2>The Spessart, Würzburg, the Steigerwald, Nuremberg</h2>
<p>At the Seligenstadt triangle, the A3 crosses into Bavaria. Near Aschaffenburg there are two noise-protection enclosures east of the town. Between Aschaffenburg and Würzburg it climbs continuously through the Spessart, with an extra fourth lane on several gradients; the highest point lies just past Rohrbrunn at about 520 metres. Near Wertheim it briefly crosses into Baden-Württemberg. At the Würzburg-West triangle the A81 branches off; through the Katzenberg tunnel it descends into the Main valley, and at the Biebelried interchange it crosses the A7. Then follow the Steigerwald, the Aischgrund and Erlangen, the A73 (Frankenschnellweg) at the Fürth/Erlangen interchange, and, in the Nuremberg Reichswald, the Nuremberg interchange with the A9. From there the A3 continues via Regensburg and Deggendorf to Passau.</p>

Tell us your direction of travel and the nearest interchange, and we'll coordinate the help.</p><p><a class="btn btn-wa" href="@@WA@@" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Send location on WhatsApp</a> <a class="btn btn-y" href="tel:+4917641956993">+49 176 41956993</a></p></div></section>

<h2>From the Hattenbach triangle to Weil am Rhein</h2>
<p>The A5 begins at the Hattenbach triangle near Bad Hersfeld, where it continues the A7's main carriageway coming from Kassel, heading south-west. In addition to the junction's normal ramps, there's a truck lane bypassing the route split. It crosses Hesse and Baden-Württemberg and ends at the Swiss border near Basel, where the Gotthard motorway A2 continues. Over this length, the route's character changes a great deal. In a breakdown, don't just say "A5": tell us the interchange, triangle or exit, and your direction of travel.</p>
<h2>The Knüll and Vogelsberg: steep, winding, four lanes</h2>
<p>The four-lane route first crosses the southern foothills of the Knüll mountains, steep and winding. At the Rimberg it reaches about 500 metres above sea level, the highest point of the entire route. A long descent follows into the Schwalm valley towards Alsfeld, then the northern Vogelsberg area, along the Felda and Ohm rivers. At the Reiskirchen triangle, the A480 branches west. At the Gambach interchange it crosses the A45. There are no ramps from north to west or vice versa there, so traffic heading west must use the A480 and the Gießen ring. Important for you: on this connection you often can't tell which direction an exit leads. Tell us your direction of travel.</p>
<h2>The Wetterau and Frankfurt: eight lanes, heavy traffic</h2>
<p>South of the Gambach interchange, the A5 has six lanes and runs along the edge of the Wetterau, past Butzbach and Bad Nauheim. Near Friedberg follows a hilly stretch by the Winterstein, the easternmost foothill of the Taunus, then a descent into the Upper Rhine lowlands. At the Bad Homburg interchange it crosses the A661, at the Frankfurt-Nordwest interchange the A66, and at the Frankfurt-West interchange the A648. The eight-lane motorway crosses the Main in the Griesheim district and reaches the A3 at the Frankfurter Kreuz. More information is at <a href="en-stadt-frankfurt.html">Towing Service Frankfurt</a> and <a href="en-autobahn-a3.html">A3</a>.</p>
<h2>The airport and Darmstadt</h2>
<p>East of Frankfurt Airport's runways, the A5 is eight lanes again, and the new Terminal 3 connects via a multi-part exit. It then runs in a straight line through woodland to Darmstadt, where the A672 leads into the city centre. At the Darmstadt interchange, the main carriageway swaps with that of the A67, which runs parallel from here on; the eight-lane section ends there, and the A5 returns to four lanes. If you break down in this area, state which of the two carriageways, A5 or A67, you're on.</p>
<h2>The Bergstraße, Heidelberg, Walldorf</h2>
<p>South of Darmstadt, the A5 runs along the Hessian and later Baden Bergstraße, with the Odenwald hills rising to the east out of the Upper Rhine rift. The parallel A67 runs several kilometres further west on the original 1930s route, closer to the Rhine. Between Heppenheim and Hemsbach it crosses the state border between Hesse and Baden-Württemberg. Near Weinheim it crosses the A659, near Dossenheim it crosses the Neckar, and near Heidelberg it crosses the A656. At the Walldorf interchange, the parallel run with the A6 ends, and the Odenwald gives way to the Kraichgau.</p>
<h2>Karlsruhe to Offenburg</h2>
<p>From Walldorf to Karlsruhe, the A5 has six lanes and carries heavy traffic. It runs through the Lußhardt forest, past Bruchsal through the Kinzig-Murg trench, and reaches Karlsruhe. After the Südtangente connection, the A8 branches east. The A5 then bypasses Karlsruhe to the south, passing Ettlingen along the edge of the northern Black Forest to Rastatt. Near Baden-Baden, the B500 forms an important link to the French motorway network, and at Appenweier the B28 is the main connection to Strasbourg. Near Offenburg, the six-lane section ends and the A5 continues with four lanes. For exits 43 to 56, see our page <a href="en-abschleppdienst-a5.html">Breakdown on the A5 between Karlsruhe and Offenburg</a>.</p>
<h2>Freiburg, the Rhine bank, Basel</h2>
<p>Near Lahr the motorway briefly swings towards the Rhine; the Europa-Park in Rust has its own exit. It then follows the Dreisam towards Freiburg, with the Kaiserstuhl rising to the west. South of Freiburg it passes the Tuniberg to Bad Krozingen and then runs through the Auwald forest right along the right bank of the Rhine, largely parallel to the French border. Past Neuenburg, a short motorway stretch branches to the French Autoroute A36. Near Bad Bellingen the motorway runs on the hillside directly above the Rhine. At the Weil am Rhein triangle, the A98 branches off as a northern bypass of Basel, followed by the joint customs facility, which lies entirely on German territory. In a breakdown near the border, additional rules apply for continuing your journey, more in our guide (German) <a href="ratgeber-panne-kehl-strassburg.html">Breakdown in the border area</a>.</p>
