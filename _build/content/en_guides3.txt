<h2>Winter tyres: what's required</h2>
<p>Germany has a situational winter-tyre requirement (§ 2 para. 3a StVO): in black ice, snow, slush, or icy/frosty conditions, you must drive on winter tyres. <li><strong>Stay warm:</strong> put on a jacket and blanket, use the heating only intermittently, and charge your phone.</li>

<p>According to the German motoring organisation ADAC, private breakdown services aren't allowed to help directly on French motorways. <section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Car Won't Start: Finding the Cause by Symptom</h1><p class="lead">The key question is: what happens when you try to start it? The answer already narrows down the cause a lot.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>First question: does the starter motor turn?</h2>
<div class="tblwrap"><table class="tbl"><thead><tr><th>What you hear</th><th>What it usually means</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Nothing, no lights on the dashboard</td><td>Flat battery or a loose terminal</td></tr>
<tr><td>A single click, nothing else</td><td>Battery too weak, starter motor, or a connection</td></tr>
<tr><td>Starter turns slowly</td><td>Weak battery, often in cold weather</td></tr>
<tr><td>Starter turns quickly, engine doesn't start</td><td>Fuel, ignition, the glow system on a diesel, or sensors</td></tr>
<tr><td>Starter doesn't respond, dashboard lights up</td><td>Immobiliser, key, starter, or start-permission system</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p>In most cases, it's a weak or flat battery. Next come the starter motor and the fuel supply.</p>
<h2>Starter motor doesn't turn</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Battery:</strong> lights, radio or central locking work poorly or not at all. Jump-starting is the first thing to try, see <a href="en-ratgeber-starthilfe-batterie.html">Jump-starting and a flat battery</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Terminals:</strong> loose or corroded terminals interrupt the current. If you can safely reach them, check them.</li>
<li><strong>Automatic:</strong> is the selector in P or N? Press the brake pedal while starting.</li>
<li><strong>Immobiliser:</strong> if the key has a faulty transponder, or the vehicle doesn't recognise it, the system refuses to start. Try the spare key. With keyless-go, a weak key-fob battery can be the cause.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Starter turns, engine doesn't start</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Fuel:</strong> tank empty? Wrong fuel? Then stop trying, see <a href="en-ratgeber-falsch-getankt.html">Wrong fuel</a>. A faulty fuel pump or a blocked filter also cuts the supply.</li>
<li><strong>Diesel engine in winter:</strong> faulty glow plugs often prevent a cold start, while the warm engine still starts.</li>
<li><strong>Ignition or sensor:</strong> the ignition system, crankshaft sensor, or control unit. This can only be checked at a garage.</li>
<li><strong>Marten damage:</strong> chewed-through cables or hoses can prevent starting.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What not to do</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Don't crank it for minutes on end. That drains the battery and can overload the starter motor. Pause a few seconds to a few minutes between attempts.</li>
<li>Don't spray starter fluid into the intake unless you know exactly what you're doing.</li>
<li>Don't push-start if your vehicle has an automatic transmission, a catalytic converter, or sensitive electronics. Many modern cars aren't designed for it.</li>
</ul>
<h2>When you should call for help</h2>
<p>If jump-starting doesn't help, warning lights are on (see <a href="en-ratgeber-warnleuchten-auto.html">Warning lights in your car</a>), there's smoke from the engine bay, or you're somewhere dangerous. The vehicle is then taken to a garage on a flatbed. For hybrids and electric cars, that's the safe route anyway, see <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>.</p>

<details><summary>Why does my diesel only fail to start in cold weather?</summary><div>On diesel engines, faulty glow plugs can prevent a cold start. A warm engine often still starts.</div></details>

=== en-ratgeber-kupplung-defekt | Clutch Failure: Symptoms and What to Do Now | Clutch pedal stuck, gears won't engage, clutch slipping: symptoms, why you shouldn't keep driving, and how the vehicle gets transported. | page ===
<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Clutch Failure: Symptoms and What to Do Now</h1><p class="lead">If no gear will engage, or the pedal stays down, continuing to drive isn't a good idea.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>How to recognise a failed clutch</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>The clutch won't disengage:</strong> the clutch disc still touches the flywheel even with the pedal pressed. Gears only engage with force, or not at all.</li>
<li><strong>The clutch pedal stays down or doesn't return.</strong> Often the cause is a sticking slave cylinder or a broken return spring.</li>
<li><strong>The pedal feels soft</strong> or suddenly has a lot of free play.</li>
<li><strong>The clutch slips:</strong> engine revs rise, but the car doesn't accelerate accordingly.</li>
<li><strong>A burning smell,</strong> jerking when pulling away, or noises when pressing the pedal.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What to do immediately</h2>
<ol>
<li>Switch on hazard lights and, as long as you can, coast to a safe spot.</li>
<li>If you can no longer change gear but still roll: let it coast, stop the vehicle, don't force a gear.</li>
<li>Apply the handbrake, put on a hi-vis vest, and get behind the crash barrier if you're on a fast road. See <a href="en-ratgeber-panne-autobahn.html">Breaking down on the motorway</a>.</li>
<li>Don't keep driving. Safe gear changes usually aren't possible any more, and you risk further damage to the transmission.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Why towing with a rope rarely works here</h2>
<p>A vehicle with a failed clutch can sometimes be towed with a rope if it's out of gear. But if the pedal is stuck or a gear is engaged, the wheels can't turn freely. On a flatbed, the vehicle is easier to move, and the transmission and drivetrain stay unstressed. Rules for towing your own vehicle are in <a href="en-ratgeber-abschleppseil-oder-abschleppdienst.html">Tow rope or towing service?</a></p>
<h2>Details about the fault</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Whether a gear is engaged and whether the vehicle can be pushed</li>
<li>Whether the pedal stays down or feels soft</li>
<li>Manual or automatic (automatic transmissions don't have a clutch in the traditional sense, see <a href="en-ratgeber-automatik-abschleppen.html">Towing an automatic car</a>)</li>
<li>Where the vehicle is and where it should go</li>
</ul>
<h2>After the transport</h2>
<p>The garage checks whether the cause is the master or slave cylinder, the return spring, the clutch disc, or the cable. Ask for a cost estimate before the repair begins. Describe how the fault first appeared, since that helps the diagnosis.</p>
<h2>Slipping, dragging, not disengaging: three different faults</h2>
<p>A failed clutch doesn't always look the same. If it <strong>slips</strong>, revs rise without the car accelerating accordingly, often with a smell of burnt friction material. If it doesn't <strong>disengage</strong> cleanly, gears only engage with force or noise, and the car can lurch forward when a gear is engaged. If it <strong>drags</strong>, noises appear when pressing or releasing the pedal. A pedal that stays on the floor after being pressed, or gives way with no resistance, points more towards the hydraulics than the friction material.</p>
<h2>Where the fault can come from</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wear on the clutch disc</strong> after many kilometres, frequent stop-and-go driving, or towing a trailer.</li>
<li><strong>Hydraulics:</strong> the master or slave cylinder or a line can develop a leak; air in the system makes the pedal feel soft.</li>
<li><strong>Cable</strong> on older vehicles: it can stretch, snap, or be mis-adjusted.</li>
<li><strong>Release bearing or dual-mass flywheel</strong>, which announce themselves through noise when the clutch is used.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pulling away without a clutch: not a good plan</h2>
<p>You sometimes hear the tip of pulling away in gear without the clutch, or changing gear without pressing it. That's an emergency workaround for extreme situations, for example getting out of a dangerous spot. As a long-term solution it strains the starter motor and transmission and can cause further damage. It's not enough to get you to a garage. If you can no longer operate the clutch safely, have the vehicle transported.</p>
<h2>Automatics and dual-clutch transmissions</h2>
<p>On vehicles with a dual-clutch or automatic transmission, a fault looks different: the vehicle jerks when pulling away, misfires, or shifts gears erratically, often with a warning message. Even then, don't keep driving on your own judgement. How an automatic vehicle is transported is covered in our guide <a href="en-ratgeber-automatik-abschleppen.html">Towing an automatic car</a>.</p>
<h2>Stopping safely</h2>
<p>Don't stay stopped with a failed clutch at a junction or in the carriageway; coast with your remaining speed to the side. Switch on hazard lights, apply the parking brake, and secure the vehicle, with a warning triangle if needed. On a slope, engage a gear or turn the wheels against the kerb so the vehicle doesn't roll away.</p>
</div></section>

<details><summary>Can I still drive to a garage with a failed clutch?</summary><div>We advise against it. Safe gear changes usually aren't possible any more, and you risk transmission damage. Have the vehicle transported.</div></details>
<details><summary>Why does my clutch pedal stay down?</summary><div>Often the slave cylinder is sticking or a spring has broken. A garage needs to check it.</div></details>
<details><summary>How do I recognise a hydraulic fault?</summary><div>The pedal feels soft, sinks slowly to the floor, or stays down. Often the brake-fluid reservoir is also low, since the brake and clutch sometimes share the same reservoir.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can I still drive to the garage?</summary><div>If gears no longer engage cleanly or the clutch slips, transport on a flatbed is safer. Continuing to drive can damage the transmission and starter motor.</div></details>
<details><summary>Does towing a trailer cause clutch wear?</summary><div>It puts more strain on the clutch, especially pulling away on a hill. Whether that's the cause in your case is for the garage to determine.</div></details>
</div></section>

<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Animal Collision: What to Do, and When the Car Should Be Towed</h1><p class="lead">An animal runs onto the road, and there's a crash. After the shock, the right order of steps matters, because it also affects whether your insurer reimburses you.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>Immediately at the scene</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Switch on hazard lights,</strong> put on a hi-vis vest, and set up a warning triangle if it's safe to do so. On fast roads, get behind the crash barrier, see <a href="en-ratgeber-panne-autobahn.html">Breaking down on the motorway</a>.</li>
<li><strong>If anyone is injured:</strong> call 112 and give first aid.</li>
<li><strong>Call the police.</strong> They document that it was an animal collision and usually inform the relevant hunter or forester.</li>
<li><strong>Take photos:</strong> the scene, the animal, the damage to the vehicle.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Don't touch the animal</h2>
<p>Don't touch injured or dead animals, partly because of the risk of rabies and infection. Leave that to the hunting leaseholder or forester. If you absolutely must move a dead animal off the road, wear gloves. Don't take a dead animal with you: that can count as poaching under German law.</p>
<h2>Wildlife-collision certificate</h2>
<p>Get a wildlife-collision certificate from the police, the forester, or the hunting leaseholder. It's your proof to the insurer that the damage was caused by an animal.</p>
<h2>Who pays?</h2>
<p>Damage to your own vehicle from game animals like deer or wild boar is usually covered by partial comprehensive insurance. Report the damage to your insurer before the vehicle is cleaned, repaired, scrapped, or sold. The exact conditions are in your policy. Breakdown cover or club membership can additionally cover the towing costs.</p>
<h2>Drive on, or have it towed?</h2>
<p>In an animal collision, the front of the car is often damaged: the radiator, headlights, bonnet. Check before continuing:</p>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Is coolant, oil or fuel leaking?</li>
<li>Do the headlights and indicators work?</li>
<li>Is the bonnet secure, or could it fly open?</li>
<li>Is the temperature gauge normal, or is there steam? See <a href="en-ratgeber-motor-ueberhitzt.html">Engine overheating</a>.</li>
<li>Are new warning lights on? See <a href="en-ratgeber-warnleuchten-auto.html">Warning lights in your car</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the answer to any of these is "yes", don't keep driving. Further criteria are in <a href="en-ratgeber-nach-unfall-fahrbereit.html">After the accident: still roadworthy or towed?</a> Electric and hybrid vehicles need special care, see <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>.</p>
<h2>What to tell a towing service</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Where you are: road, section, car park, or on a motorway the direction and exit</li>
<li>How the vehicle is positioned and whether parts are loose or lying on the road</li>
<li>Whether the police are already there or on their way</li>

<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Car Stalled in Water: Don't Restart It</h1><p class="lead">After driving through water, the engine stutters or cuts out. The most important rule first: don't try to start it again.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>What hydrolock is</h2>
<p>If the engine draws in water instead of air, that's called hydrolock. Water can't be compressed, so the engine stops abruptly. This can bend connecting rods, break valves, and in the worst case crack the engine block. Typical signs are an engine that won't restart after driving through water, or one that cuts out mid-drive.</p>
<h2>Do this immediately</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Switch off the engine if it's still running,</strong> and don't restart it. A start attempt can significantly increase the damage.</li>
<li><strong>Switch on hazard lights</strong> and get out if the spot is safe. If the water is rising, leave the vehicle before the doors can no longer be opened.</li>
<li><strong>Don't wade through moving water.</strong> Even shallow water can sweep you off your feet.</li>
<li><strong>Call for help:</strong> 112 if anyone is in danger, otherwise a towing service.</li>
<li><strong>Take photos:</strong> the water level, the vehicle, the surroundings. You'll need them for your insurer.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Why the car shouldn't drive itself</h2>
<p>A vehicle that has drawn in water can look fine on the outside. The damage is inside the engine. Don't tow it with the driven wheels turning, since that spins the engine over. Transport on a flatbed is the safe solution, especially for electric and hybrid vehicles. For those, water can also affect high-voltage components and connectors, see <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>.</p>
<h2>Informing your insurer</h2>
<p>Report the damage to your insurer before the vehicle is repaired. Whether and how much is paid depends on your policy and the circumstances, for example whether it was a flood or a deliberate drive through flooded roads. The garage should document the damage and establish the cause.</p>
<h2>What to tell the towing service</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Where the vehicle is and how deep the water is</li>
<li>Whether the water is still rising or the road is closed</li>
<li>Vehicle and drivetrain</li>
<li>Whether the engine was started again after contact with water</li>
<li>Whether the doors and locks still work</li>
</ul>
<p>If your car is in water in an underground garage or underpass, our guide <a href="ratgeber-tiefgarage-parkhaus-liegengeblieben.html">Underground garage and car park</a> can help.</p>
<h2>Preventing it</h2>
<p>Don't drive through flooded roads. If it's unavoidable, drive slowly and steadily so no bow wave forms, and keep the engine revs constant.</p>
<h2>Wading depth and the air intake</h2>
<p>Every vehicle has a maximum wading depth, stated by the manufacturer in the handbook. It mainly depends on where the engine draws in air. If the intake sits low, even a puddle can be enough for water to reach the engine. Water can't be compressed, so the engine can be seriously damaged when it draws it in, with connecting rods breaking in the worst case. If the engine stalls while driving through water, that's often the exact moment the damage happens or is about to happen.</p>
<h2>What water does to the electrics and electronics</h2>
<p>Modern vehicles have control units, wiring harnesses and sensors located throughout the car, some in footwells or under seats. Water can corrode connectors and damage control units, even if the engine itself was untouched. On hybrid and electric vehicles, the high-voltage system is also affected, and special caution applies, see <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>. A vehicle that's been standing in water shouldn't be put back into use until a garage has checked it.</p>
<h2>The oil reveals it</h2>
<p>A sign of water inside the engine is milky-grey oil on the dipstick or in the filler neck. Rough running or water in the air-filter housing are also signs. Tell the garage about these findings, since they determine the scope of the repair.</p>
<h2>Don't push-start or roll-start it</h2>
<p>The vehicle shouldn't be pushed or roll-started to start the engine. The momentum can damage the engine further if water is sitting in the cylinders. Automatic transmissions shouldn't simply be towed either, see <a href="en-ratgeber-automatik-abschleppen.html">Towing an automatic car</a>. The safe route is transport on a flatbed.</p>
<h2>After the water: what to document</h2>
<ul>
<li>Photos of the vehicle and surroundings showing the water level, as far as it's safe to take them.</li>
<li>Time, place and weather conditions.</li>
<li>Whether the engine was running when the water got in, and when it stopped.</li>
<li>Invoices for transport and inspection, since they can matter for your insurer.</li>
</ul>
</div></section>

<details><summary>What does hydrolock mean?</summary><div>The engine draws in water instead of air and stops abruptly. This can damage connecting rods, valves and the engine block.</div></details>
<details><summary>How deep can I drive through water?</summary><div>The manufacturer sets this as the wading depth in the handbook. If in doubt, don't drive through, since the depth of the road is hard to judge.</div></details>
