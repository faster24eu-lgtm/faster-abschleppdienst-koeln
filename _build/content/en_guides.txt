<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>What Does a Towing Service Cost?</h1><p class="lead">Prices vary widely. Here's what they depend on, and how to avoid surprises.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>Reference prices in Germany</h2>
<p>The following figures are market reference prices from 2026 price surveys. They show the rough order of magnitude but don't replace an actual quote.</p>

<p>For individual cities, the portal kostenfinder.com (2026) gives the following averages: Karlsruhe around €219 (range about €102 to €383), Freiburg around €223, Stuttgart around €240.</p>
<h2>Why the range is so wide</h2>
<p>A towing service isn't a product with a fixed price. Two callouts in the same city can differ a lot: in one case a car sits on level ground in a car park and still rolls, in another it's in a ditch at night after an accident. Companies also calculate differently. Some charge the callout and mileage separately, others quote a flat price. That's why an average tells you little about your own situation.</p>
<h2>What the price depends on</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Distance:</strong> the callout and the trip to the destination. A short trip to the nearest garage is cheaper than a long journey.</li>
<li><strong>Time of day:</strong> surcharges are common at night, on weekends and on public holidays.</li>
<li><strong>Vehicle:</strong> weight, length, drivetrain. Electric cars and vans often need more effort.</li>
<li><strong>Condition:</strong> a vehicle that no longer rolls, is in a ditch, or is in an underground garage makes more work than one that's simply loaded up.</li>
<li><strong>Location:</strong> special conditions apply on the motorway, for example securing the scene.</li>
<li><strong>Waiting time and extra services:</strong> for example recovery, oil-absorbent material, or a second callout.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Roadside help on the spot, or towing?</h2>
<p>Often, help on the spot is the cheaper solution. Jump-starting a flat battery, a canister of fuel, or a wheel change usually costs less than transport. A reputable company will tell you whether the problem can be solved on the spot before sending a vehicle out. If a provider wants to tow immediately without asking questions, ask why.</p>
<h2>Questions to ask before you commit</h2>
<ol>
<li>Is this a fixed price or an estimate?</li>
<li>Does it include the callout, loading, and all mileage?</li>
<li>Is there a surcharge for nights, weekends or public holidays?</li>
<li>Is VAT included?</li>
<li>How can I pay?</li>
<li>Exactly where will the vehicle be taken?</li>
</ol>
<p>Get the price in writing where possible, for example over WhatsApp. A reputable provider states it before they drive out.</p>
<h2>Our fixed price (Karlsruhe to Offenburg region)</h2>
<p>In the Karlsruhe to Offenburg region, our fixed price for towing within a city starts at <strong>€129</strong>. It applies to shorter distances; we tell you the exact amount on the phone based on your location, destination and vehicle.</p>
<h2>When someone else pays</h2>
<p>After an accident that wasn't your fault, the other driver's liability insurance usually covers the towing costs. Breakdown cover or membership in an automobile club can also cover the help. Check your documents before you pay. More in our guide <a href="en-ratgeber-unfall-abschleppkosten.html">Towing costs after an accident</a>.</p>
<h2>Checking the invoice</h2>
<p>Compare the invoice with the price you were quoted. If it lists an amount nobody mentioned beforehand, ask for a breakdown. In a dispute over an inflated invoice after an accident, the at-fault party's insurer can be brought in, see our accident guide.</p>

=== en-ratgeber-panne-autobahn | Breaking Down on the Motorway: What to Do? The Right Steps | Breaking down on the motorway: hazard lights, hi-vis vest, warning triangle, emergency phone, towing service. The steps in the right order, with distances. | page ===
<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Breaking Down on the Motorway: What to Do?</h1><p class="lead">Stay calm and follow these steps in order. Your safety comes before the vehicle.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>The steps</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Switch on your hazard lights</strong> and coast as far right as you can with the momentum you have. If possible, aim for the next rest area or exit, otherwise the hard shoulder.</li>
<li><strong>Put on a hi-vis vest</strong> before getting out. Get out on the passenger side, away from moving traffic.</li>
<li><strong>Get everyone out</strong> and move behind the crash barrier. Don't leave children or pets in the vehicle.</li>
<li><strong>Set up a warning triangle</strong>, but only if it's safe to do so. On the motorway, a rough guideline is at least 150 metres, more if possible. Walk there from behind the crash barrier.</li>
<li><strong>Call for help:</strong> 112 if anyone is injured. For a breakdown with no injuries, call a towing service or your automobile club's or insurer's roadside assistance.</li>
<li><strong>Don't repair it yourself</strong>, and don't change a wheel on the hard shoulder.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Why the order matters</h2>
<p>Most serious accidents after a breakdown aren't caused by the breakdown itself, but by what follows: people standing on the hard shoulder, working on the vehicle, or walking around without a hi-vis vest. A vehicle can be replaced, you can't. Leave the car as quickly as possible and get behind the crash barrier.</p>
<h2>Distance for the warning triangle</h2>
<table class="tbl"><thead><tr><th>Road type</th><th>Reference distance</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>In town</td><td>about 50 metres</td></tr>
<tr><td>Country road</td><td>about 100 metres</td></tr>
<tr><td>Motorway</td><td>at least 150 metres, up to about 400 metres</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p class="fine">German traffic law (§ 15 StVO) requires a warning sign at a sufficient distance. The figures given are common reference values. Behind a bend or a crest, place the triangle further away.</p>
<h2>Reporting your location</h2>
<p>What matters is the motorway (e.g. A5), your direction of travel, the last exit, and, if visible, the distance marker on the signs. The arrows on the guide posts point to the nearest emergency phone. On your phone you can share your location via WhatsApp. If you use the emergency phone, you don't need to explain anything: it's connected to a control centre that already knows your location.</p>
<h2>Why you can't always choose the towing service</h2>
<p>If the police attend, or you've used the emergency phone, a contracted company is often assigned. You can state your preference, but the company doesn't always decide based on it. For a simple breakdown without police involvement, you can choose freely.</p>
<h2>Towing from the motorway</h2>
<p>When towing a vehicle that has broken down on the motorway, the motorway must be left at the next exit (§ 15a para. 1 StVO). Both vehicles must have their hazard lights on while towing. Motorcycles may not be towed; they must be transported.</p>
<h2>Stuck in a traffic jam</h2>
<p>In slow-moving traffic, an emergency lane must be formed. A stranded vehicle must not block it. Move as far to the side as you can.</p>
<h2>Special cases</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>In a tunnel:</strong> hazard lights on, engine off, leave the vehicle, head to the nearest emergency point, if it's safe to do so.</li>
<li><strong>Warning light in the dashboard:</strong> for red symbols like oil pressure or coolant, stop immediately, see <a href="ratgeber-warnleuchten-auto.html">Warning lights in your car</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Electric car:</strong> if there's smoke or battery damage, keep your distance and call 112. See <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>.</li>
<li><strong>With children or pets:</strong> get everyone to safety first, then make phone calls.</li>
<li><strong>At night or in fog:</strong> always get behind the crash barrier, wear the hi-vis vest, and make the vehicle as visible as possible.</li>
</ul>
<p>Detailed help for the A5 between Karlsruhe and Offenburg: <a href="en-abschleppdienst-a5.html">Towing Service A5</a>. For Cologne: <a href="en-pannenhilfe-koelner-autobahnring.html">Cologne Motorway Ring</a>.</p>
</div></section>

<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Towing Costs After an Accident: Who Pays?</h1><p class="lead">If you didn't cause the accident, the other driver's insurer usually pays.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<div class="note"><p>This is a general overview, not legal advice. In a dispute, a lawyer or your insurer can help.</p></div>
<h2>The basic rule</h2>
<p>If someone else is responsible for the accident, their vehicle liability insurance usually covers the towing costs. Towing costs count among the damages that get reimbursed.</p>
<h2>Who chooses the towing service?</h2>
<p>As the injured party, you may choose the company yourself. You don't have to compare prices in an emergency. Right after an accident, there's usually no time for that anyway. In such an urgent situation, the at-fault party owes the costs actually incurred, regardless of whether the price matches the average.</p>
<h2>Where may the vehicle go?</h2>
<p>The duty to minimise damages applies: you should keep costs as low as reasonably possible. In practice that usually means the vehicle goes to the nearest suitable garage. A long trip to your regular garage can mean the insurer won't cover the extra cost. Discuss the destination with us and with your insurer beforehand. Whether the car may even be driven at all is covered in <a href="ratgeber-nach-unfall-fahrbereit.html">After the accident: still roadworthy or towed?</a></p>
<h2>If the insurer thinks the price is too high</h2>
<p>Usually the person who ordered the tow, meaning you, pays the invoice first. If the insurer thinks it's inflated, it can ask you to assign your claim against the towing company to it and handle the dispute itself. Then you don't have to argue about it.</p>
<h2>If you caused the accident</h2>
<p>Then you pay first yourself, unless your comprehensive cover, breakdown cover, or automobile club membership covers it. A quick look at your policy or a call to your insurer will clarify this.</p>
<h2>If both sides share the blame</h2>
<p>If those involved share the blame, the costs are also split, according to the ratio the insurers determine. In that case, keep all receipts and pay the invoice yourself so you can claim it back.</p>
<h2>What to do after an accident</h2>
<ol>
<li>Secure the scene: hazard lights, hi-vis vest, warning triangle.</li>
<li>Call 112 if anyone is injured.</li>
<li>Call the police if needed, especially for injuries, a dispute over fault, or a hit-and-run.</li>
<li>Exchange details: name, address, licence plate, insurer.</li>
<li>Take photos: vehicles, damage, location, road.</li>
<li>Choose a towing service and state the destination.</li>
<li>Keep the invoice and receipt.</li>
</ol>
<h2>What you should keep as evidence</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Photos of the vehicle, the accident scene, and the damage</li>
<li>The other driver's name and insurer; call the police if needed</li>
<li>The invoice and proof that it's been paid</li>
<li>The towing destination and the reason you chose it</li>
</ul>
</div></section>
<section class="section grey"><div class="wrap narrow"><h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<details><summary>Do I have to use the towing service the police name?</summary><div>If the police call in a contracted service, that's a different situation. But you may still state your preference. For minor accidents without police involvement, you can choose freely.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can I have the car taken straight to my regular garage?</summary><div>That's possible, but after an accident that wasn't your fault, the insurer usually only covers the trip to the nearest suitable garage.</div></details>
<details><summary>Do I have to compare prices?</summary><div>No. In the emergency after an accident, you don't need to get quotes.</div></details>
</div></section>

<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Towing an Electric Car: What You Should Know</h1><p class="lead">Electric cars are transported most safely on a flatbed vehicle. Here's why.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>Why not just pull it?</h2>
<p>An electric motor can work as a generator. That's the principle behind regenerative braking: the vehicle recovers energy while braking. If the driven wheels turn while being towed, the motor generates voltage that the powered-down control electronics can't handle. In the worst case, this causes damage. That's why the driven axle shouldn't touch the ground.</p>
<h2>The safe way: a flatbed vehicle</h2>
<p>A vehicle with a loading bed transports the EV entirely off the ground. This method is considered the safest and is generally accepted by manufacturers. Whether and how a specific model may be moved another way is stated in the owner's manual. If in doubt, ask the manufacturer or your garage.</p>
<h2>Fully electric, hybrid, plug-in hybrid</h2>
<p>Hybrids also have an electric motor and sometimes a high-voltage battery. What applies to pure electric cars can therefore also apply, depending on the model, to plug-in hybrids and other hybrid vehicles. Tell us on the phone what drivetrain your vehicle has. If you don't know, tell us the make, model and year.</p>
<h2>What to tell us</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Make, model and drivetrain: fully electric, hybrid, or plug-in hybrid</li>
<li>Whether the vehicle still rolls or is locked (on some models the parking lock or electric parking brake can't be released without power)</li>
<li>Whether there was an accident or a high-voltage battery warning</li>
<li>Where it is: underground garage, charging point, street</li>
<li>Whether the vehicle is plugged into a charging point</li>
</ul>
<h2>Flat 12-volt battery</h2>
<p>Electric cars also have a small 12-volt battery for electronics, locks and brakes. If it's flat, the vehicle sometimes can't be unlocked or started, even though the main battery still has charge. Jump-starting is then possible depending on the model, but only at the connection points specified by the manufacturer. <section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Jump-Starting and a Flat Battery</h1><p class="lead">Car just clicks, or does nothing at all? Usually the battery is to blame. It can often be fixed on the spot.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>How to recognise a flat battery</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Turning the key or pressing the start button does nothing, or just clicks</li>
<li>The starter motor turns very slowly</li>
<li>The dashboard lights are dim or go out when you try to start</li>
<li>Central locking no longer responds</li>
</ul>
<h2>Why the battery goes flat</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Cold:</strong> in frost a battery delivers less power, but the engine needs more.</li>
<li><strong>Short trips:</strong> driving only short distances barely recharges the battery.</li>
<li><strong>Age:</strong> capacity fades after a few years.</li>
<li><strong>Lights or accessories left on:</strong> a forgotten parking light is enough.</li>
<li><strong>Long standing time:</strong> even a parked vehicle uses power, for example for the alarm and control units.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Jump-starting step by step</h2>
<p>For a jump-start you need a second vehicle with a similar battery voltage and jump-start cables. An overview:</p>
<ol>
<li>Both engines off, ignition off. Don't let the clamps touch each other.</li>
<li>Red cable to the positive terminal of the flat battery, then to the positive terminal of the donor battery.</li>
<li>Black cable to the negative terminal of the donor battery, the other end to a bare metal part on the engine block or an earth point of the stranded vehicle, not to the negative terminal of the flat battery.</li>
<li>Start the donor vehicle and let it run briefly, then start the stranded vehicle.</li>
<li>Remove the cables in reverse order. Then drive for a longer stretch so the battery recharges.</li>
</ol>
<div class="note"><p>On hybrid and electric cars, start-stop systems and modern vehicles, the connection points are often specifically designated. Read the owner's manual before attaching cables.</p></div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Cables swapped: positive to negative can destroy the electronics.</li>
<li>Black cable straight to the negative terminal of the flat battery: this can cause sparks at the battery.</li>
<li>Running it too briefly: switching off right after starting means you'll soon be stuck again.</li>
<li>Jump-starting an obviously damaged or frozen battery: don't do this.</li>
</ul>
<h2>When you should call us</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>You don't have a second vehicle or cables</li>
<li>You're somewhere dangerous (motorway, tunnel, underground garage)</li>
<li>The vehicle still won't start despite jump-starting; there may be another fault</li>
<li>It's a hybrid or electric car</li>
</ul>
<p>We try jump-starting the vehicle on the spot first. If that doesn't work, we take it to a garage.</p>
<h2>Preventing it next time</h2>
<p>Have the battery checked, especially if it's already a few years old. Avoid repeated short trips in winter, or recharge the battery occasionally with a charger. Breakdown cover can also be worthwhile, since it covers roadside assistance.</p>
</div></section>
<section class="section grey"><div class="wrap narrow"><h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<details><summary>How long do I need to drive after jump-starting?</summary><div>A longer drive is better than a short trip, because the alternator needs time to recharge the battery. Have the battery checked afterwards.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can jump-starting damage the car?</summary><div>Yes, if connected incorrectly. Follow the owner's manual, or let us do it.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can I just try again in the cold?</summary><div>Several attempts in a row drain the battery further. Better to wait a few minutes or get a jump-start.</div></details>

<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Wrong Fuel: What Now?</h1><p class="lead">Diesel in a petrol car, or petrol in a diesel: if you notice the mistake early, the damage can usually be kept to a minimum.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>Do this immediately</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Stop refuelling.</strong> As soon as you realise you've used the wrong pump, stop.</li>
<li><strong>Don't start the engine or switch on the ignition.</strong> If the fuel is in the tank but hasn't reached the fuel line yet, the damage is usually small.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the door closed if possible.</strong> On many modern vehicles, opening the driver's door already starts the fuel pre-supply pump, which can carry the wrong mixture further.</li>
<li><strong>Move the vehicle to a safe spot,</strong> for example by rolling or pushing it away from the pump.</li>
<li><strong>Call for help:</strong> a garage or towing service can take the vehicle somewhere the tank can be pumped out.</li>
</ol>
<h2>If the engine has already run</h2>
<p>If you only notice the mistake while driving, for example because the engine stutters or warning lights come on, switch off the engine immediately, coast to a safe spot, and don't drive any further. The longer the wrong fuel mixture runs through the system, the greater the potential damage to the injection system, up to and including the high-pressure pump, injectors and fuel lines.</p>
<h2>What happens at the garage</h2>
<p>The tank is pumped out and, if needed, flushed. Depending on how far the wrong fuel travelled, the lines and filters are also checked. It's worth telling the garage exactly when you noticed the mistake and whether the engine was running.</p>
<h2>Who pays?</h2>
<p>The mistake is usually yours. Manufacturers or vehicle insurance generally don't cover a wrongly filled tank. With breakdown cover, part of the cost for towing and pumping out can be reimbursed. Check your policy or call your club before you pay.</p>
<h2>Preventing it</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Read the sign on the pump nozzle and the label inside the fuel cap.</li>
<li>Diesel nozzles are usually thicker and don't fit many petrol cars. That's a clue, but not a guaranteed safeguard.</li>
<li>If you have a rental vehicle, ask about the fuel type at handover.</li>
<li>If unsure: don't refuel, ask first.</li>
</ul>
</div></section>

<details><summary>Why shouldn't I open the door?</summary><div>On many modern vehicles, the fuel pre-supply pump starts as soon as the driver's door opens. You can avoid this by leaving it closed.</div></details>

=== en-ratgeber-abschleppseil-oder-abschleppdienst | Tow Rope or Towing Service? Rules and Risks | Towing your own car: rules for ropes and bars, power steering, brake assist, hazard lights, and when a professional is safer. | page ===
<section class="page-head"><div class="wrap narrow"><p class="eyebrow">Guides</p><h1>Tow Rope or Towing Service?</h1><p class="lead">Towing your own car is allowed, but not without risk. Here are the rules and the limits.</p></div></section>
<section class="section"><div class="wrap narrow copy">
<h2>The most important rules</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Hazard lights:</strong> both vehicles must have their hazard lights on (§ 15a para. 3 StVO).</li>
<li><strong>Distance:</strong> the rope must not be longer than about five metres and should be marked in the middle with a red cloth.</li>
<li><strong>Motorway:</strong> a vehicle that has broken down on the motorway must be towed off at the next exit (§ 15a para. 1 StVO).</li>
<li><strong>Motorcycles:</strong> motorcycles may not be towed (§ 15a para. 4 StVO).</li>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> drive slowly. About 40 km/h is a common guideline.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What makes towing difficult</h2>
<p>With the engine off, power steering and brake assist don't work. The stranded vehicle then needs significantly more force to steer and brake. The rope must always stay taut, otherwise jerks and collisions can happen. Anyone unfamiliar with this easily underestimates it. And whoever sits in the back has no control over what's happening at the front.</p>
<h2>When you shouldn't use a rope</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li><strong>Electric and hybrid cars:</strong> regenerative braking can cause damage, see <a href="en-ratgeber-e-auto-abschleppen.html">Towing an electric car</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Automatic transmission:</strong> many models can only be towed without the engine running under restrictions, see <a href="ratgeber-automatik-abschleppen.html">Towing an automatic car</a>.</li>
<li><strong>A locked wheel:</strong> a wheel that doesn't turn drags across the road surface. See <a href="ratgeber-handbremse-loest-sich-nicht.html">Handbrake won't release</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Accident damage:</strong> damaged axles, steering or brakes make towing with a rope dangerous.</li>
<li><strong>Heavy traffic, motorways, mountains:</strong> a towing service is safer here.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Tow bar or rope?</h2>
<p>A tow bar keeps the vehicles at a fixed distance and makes braking easier, because the towed vehicle can't run into the back of the towing vehicle. It must fit the vehicle and be attached to the designated towing eyes.</p>
<h2>If you do it yourself anyway: the process</h2>
<ol>
<li>Secure both vehicles: hazard lights on, wear a hi-vis vest.</li>
<li>Find the towing eye or designated attachment points on both vehicles (owner's manual).</li>
<li>Tighten the rope as the towing vehicle pulls away, but not abruptly.</li>
<li>Switch on the ignition in the towed vehicle so the steering doesn't lock.</li>
<li>Short distance, low speed, brake well in advance.</li>
<li>At the destination, only release the rope once both vehicles have stopped.</li>
</ol>
<h2>The advantage of a towing service</h2>
<p>A towing service loads the vehicle onto a flatbed. This spares the transmission, drivetrain and electronics, and the vehicle doesn't need to steer or brake at all. And you don't have to sit behind the wheel of a vehicle you probably shouldn't be driving anyway. Whether damage from towing your own car privately is covered by insurance is also often unclear.</p>
</div></section>
<section class="section grey"><div class="wrap narrow"><h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<details><summary>Do I need a licence to tow?</summary><div>Whoever drives the towing vehicle needs the appropriate licence. Someone able to steer and brake must also sit at the wheel of the towed vehicle.</div></details>
<details><summary>Can I have a friend tow me?</summary><div>Yes, if all the rules are followed. But consider the risks, and that damage to the towing vehicle or an accident can raise insurance questions.</div></details>
