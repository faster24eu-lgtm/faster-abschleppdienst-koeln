Car Stuck in Limp Mode: Power Gone, What Now?

The car barely accelerates any more, and the engine light is on. This usually isn't a total failure, but a protective reaction from the engine control unit.

What limp mode is

If the control unit detects a fault or implausible sensor readings, it switches to a limp-mode program. It throttles power, engine speed, or boost pressure to prevent greater engine damage. The vehicle stays driveable, but with restrictions.

How to recognise limp mode

  • Noticeable loss of power, the vehicle barely accelerates
  • Engine speed is limited, often around 3,000 rpm
  • Sluggish response to the accelerator
  • The amber engine warning light, see Warning lights in your car

Possible causes

Common causes are faulty sensors, fuel-supply problems, a failed turbocharger, or faults in the exhaust system. Typical triggers include boost pressure or the turbo, EGR and the particulate filter, the air-mass sensor, the throttle body, or rail pressure. On diesels, empty AdBlue can also be the reason, see AdBlue empty.

What to do

  1. Ease off and stop safely. Switch on hazard lights, coast to a safe spot.
  2. Switch off the engine and restart it. A restart often temporarily clears limp mode. But the cause stays in the fault memory.
  3. Have the fault memory read out as soon as possible. Without a diagnosis, the cause can't be reliably identified.
  4. Don't ignore it. Limp mode protects the engine, but the underlying cause remains.

Keep driving, or get towed?

In limp mode, the vehicle can usually still reach the nearest garage. But that only makes sense on a calm drive along a safe route. On the motorway, on inclines, and in heavy traffic, a vehicle with little power becomes a hazard itself. In those cases, transport is the better choice. Also have the vehicle transported if:

  • a red warning light is on, for example oil pressure, coolant, or the brakes
  • there's smoke, a burning smell, or unusual noises, see Engine overheating
  • the vehicle keeps stalling, see Engine cutting out while driving
  • you feel unsafe

Details that help with diagnosis

  • Make, model, drivetrain (petrol, diesel, hybrid)
  • Which lights are on and whether there's a message on the display
  • Whether a restart helped
  • Where you are, and on the motorway the direction and exit

Petrol, diesel, turbo: different triggers

Limp mode protects the engine and exhaust system by having the control unit limit power and revs. On turbocharged engines, a fault in the boost-pressure control or a leaking intercooler hose can trigger it. Diesel vehicles add faults in the exhaust system, such as the particulate filter or EGR. On petrol engines, ignition coils and sensors are common candidates. Only reading the fault memory reveals the actual cause in your case.

Engine off, engine on: does that help?

Limp mode often disappears for a while after switching off and restarting, because the control unit resets the fault message. That's not a repair, just a postponement. If limp mode returns the next time you accelerate, the cause is still there. Don't assume the car is "back to normal" just because the engine warning light stays on.

Reading the fault memory

The cause is stored in the fault memory. Garages read it via the OBD port. Simple adapters with a smartphone app can also show codes, but they don't replace a proper diagnosis. Note for the garage: when limp mode occurred, under what load, with a cold or warm engine, and what message appeared in the instrument cluster.

Drive on briefly, or get towed?

  • Continuing to drive can be reasonable for a short distance with mild power reduction and no red warning light, to reach a safe spot.
  • Stop and have it transported if there's a red warning light, engine jerking, a smell of fuel or burnt oil, smoke, or unusual noises.
  • On motorways with heavily limited power, continuing to drive is dangerous, since you're significantly slower than surrounding traffic. Use the next exit or the hard shoulder and follow the steps in Breaking down on the motorway.

After limp mode

Have the vehicle checked soon. Ignored faults, such as a faulty boost-pressure valve or a leaking air hose, can lead to expensive follow-on damage to the turbocharger or catalytic converter. That's especially true if the engine light flashes amber, or the engine runs unevenly at idle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is limp mode dangerous?
It protects the engine. It becomes dangerous if the vehicle has too little power in heavy traffic or on a hill.
Does limp mode go away after a restart?
Often, for a short time. The cause stays stored and limp mode can return. Have the vehicle checked.
Is limp mode the same as the engine warning light?
No. The engine warning light indicates a fault; limp mode is the control unit's protective reaction with reduced power. Both can occur together.
Can I reset limp mode myself?
A restart or disconnecting the battery can clear the message, but not fix the cause. The fault usually comes back.
How does limp mode affect continuing to drive?
Power is reduced, usually with a limited engine speed too. On country roads and motorways, that's risky, since you're slower than surrounding traffic.
